# Adapting Teresa Torres' Opportunity Assessment to Different Contexts

Teresa Torres' framework in *Continuous Discovery Habits* proposes assessing sibling opportunities using four categories: **Opportunity Sizing**, **Market Factors**, **Company Factors**, and **Customer Factors**. These were originally framed for tech companies building products, but the underlying logic applies to any organization.

This reference explains how to adapt each category to non-corporate contexts.

## 1. Default — Tech Company / Startup

Use Teresa Torres' original framing:

| Category | What to assess |
|---|---|
| **Opportunity Sizing** | How many customers affected? How often? Revenue impact? |
| **Market Factors** | Market trends, competitive landscape, regulatory changes, timing |
| **Company Factors** | Strategic alignment, team strengths, technical feasibility, cost |
| **Customer Factors** | Customer importance, satisfaction with current solution, willingness to pay |

---

## 2. Union / Sindicat (e.g., Sindicat de Llogateres)

Unions are mission-driven organizations where "customers" are members/affiliates, "market" is the external political-legal context, and "company" is the organization's internal capacity and political dynamics.

| Original | Adapted | What to assess |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity Sizing** | **Dimensionament** | How many people affected (members, volunteers, staff)? How often? Distinguish "many people affected occasionally" from "few people affected constantly." Is it a bottleneck that blocks other processes? |
| **Market Factors** | **Factors de context extern** | Legal landscape (housing law, judicial trends). Media attention windows. Growth of the social movement. Actions by landlords/property companies. Relationship with other unions or movements. External urgency. |
| **Company Factors** | **Factors del sindicat** | Alignment with the union's mission and values. Internal political dynamics (consensus, controversy). Volunteer/staff capacity and burnout risk. Existing knowledge and tools. Cost of implementation relative to budget (often near-zero). Sustainability of the organization. |
| **Customer Factors** | **Factors de la persona usuària** | Who is the primary affected person? (member/striker, delegate, lawyer, treasurer, secretary). How much does this matter to them? How satisfied are they with the current manual workaround? What's the emotional dimension (fear, anxiety, frustration, solidarity)? |

**Key differences from corporate context:**
- **No revenue metric** — Impact is measured in people helped, time saved for volunteers, legal protection, movement growth
- **Burnout is a strategic risk** — Overloaded volunteers quit, and they're harder to replace than employees
- **Political alignment matters** — Some improvements are politically uncontroversial (everyone agrees automation is good), others may generate debate (e.g., AI replacing human contact)
- **Mission alignment is non-negotiable** — The union exists to fight for housing rights, not to be operationally efficient. Efficiency serves the mission, not the other way around
- **"Customers" are also the organization** — Members are both the users and the reason the union exists. There's no separation between user satisfaction and organizational mission.

## 3. NGO / Nonprofit

| Original | Adapted | What to assess |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity Sizing** | **Dimensionament** | Beneficiaries affected. Frequency. Severity of need. |
| **Market Factors** | **Factors externs** | Funding landscape. Donor priorities. Regulatory environment. Public awareness. Collaboration opportunities with other orgs. |
| **Company Factors** | **Factors de l'organització** | Mission alignment. Staff/volunteer capacity. Existing infrastructure. Funding constraints. Board priorities. |
| **Customer Factors** | **Factors del beneficiari** | Who is the beneficiary? How urgent is their need? How do they currently cope? What barriers exist (access, literacy, trust)? |

---

## 4. Cooperative

| Original | Adapted | What to assess |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity Sizing** | **Dimensionament** | Members affected. Economic impact per member. Frequency. |
| **Market Factors** | **Factors de mercat** | Market competition. Regulatory advantages for coops. Sector trends. Intercooperation opportunities. |
| **Company Factors** | **Factors de la cooperativa** | Democratic decision-making (assembly approval needed?). Member capacity. Financial reserves. Values alignment. Worker wellbeing. |
| **Customer Factors** | **Factors del soci/client** | Member-owners as users. Client satisfaction. Member participation and engagement. |

---

## 5. Public Sector

| Original | Adapted | What to assess |
|---|---|---|
| **Opportunity Sizing** | **Dimensionament** | Citizens/users affected. Service frequency. Equity considerations (who is underserved?). |
| **Market Factors** | **Factors externs** | Political mandate. Legislative requirements. Budget cycles. Public opinion. Interoperability with other agencies. |
| **Company Factors** | **Factors institucionals** | Mandate alignment. Staff capacity. IT infrastructure. Procurement constraints. Inter-departmental coordination. |
| **Customer Factors** | **Factors del ciutadà** | Citizen needs and expectations. Digital divide. Accessibility requirements. Trust in government. |

## 6. General Adaptation Principles

When adapting to any context:

1. **Keep the comparative logic.** The power of Torres' framework is comparing siblings against each other, not scoring in absolute terms. This works in any context.

2. **Rename but preserve the structure.** The four categories (sizing, external, internal, user) are universal. Rename them to use the organization's natural language.

3. **Identify the equivalent of "revenue."** In a company it's money. In a union it's members mobilized and rights won. In an NGO it's beneficiaries served. In a coop it's member value. The sizing metric changes but the logic is the same.

4. **Surface the human cost.** In non-corporate contexts, the "cost" of not addressing an opportunity is often human (burnout, exclusion, anxiety, injustice) rather than financial. Make this explicit in the analysis.

5. **Write in prose, not tables.** The qualitative narrative format works especially well for non-corporate contexts where the nuances and tensions are hard to capture in numbers. A union's opportunity assessment needs to express things like "this is politically uncontroversial but operationally complex" or "this matters deeply to the affected person but only affects 2 people" — prose does this naturally.
