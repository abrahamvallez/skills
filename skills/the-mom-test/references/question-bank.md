# Question bank — good, bad, and how to fix them

Source: *The Mom Test* (Ch. 1 "Good question / bad question", Ch. 2, Ch. 3, Ch. 5). Paraphrased. Spanish renderings are the skill's own convenience, not from the book.

## 1. The book's reference list (Ch. 1)

| Question | Verdict | Why | Fix / use |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Do you think it's a good idea?" | ❌ Awful | Only the market can say; yours is an opinion with high false-positive risk (unless the person is a deep industry expert). | Ask them to show how they do it today; what they love/hate; what else they tried; are they searching for a replacement; where they lose money; is there a budget. Then judge the idea yourself. |
| "Would you buy a product which did X?" | ❌ Bad | Hypothetical + future, from optimistic people who want to please you. Almost always "yes". | How do they solve X now, what does it cost (money, time), talk me through the last time it came up; if they haven't solved it, why not — did they search and find options wanting, or not care enough to Google it? |
| "How much would you pay for X?" | ❌ Bad (worse: the number feels rigorous) | Same as above, plus false precision. | What does the problem cost them? What do they pay now? What budget is allocated? Or, if far enough along, ask for money (deposit / pre-order). |
| "What would your dream product do?" | ⚠️ Sort-of OK, only with follow-ups | Value is in the *why* behind the wishes, not in collecting features. | "Why do you want that?", what constraints/motivations sit behind it. |
| "Why do you bother?" | ✅ Good | Moves from perceived problem to the real one; reveals motivation (e.g. "so we all work off the latest version" → closer to Dropbox than a chat tool). | Use after any workaround/behaviour you don't fully understand. |
| "What are the implications of that?" | ✅ Good | Separates will-pay-to-solve problems from kind-of-annoying ones; gives a pricing signal. | Use when someone describes a problem with big emotion — the "DISASTER" may be absorbed by interns. |
| "Talk me through the last time that happened." | ✅ Good | Show-don't-tell; actions beat opinions; reveals workflow, tools, people, constraints. Best of all: watch them do it. | Core question. Follow with "and then?", "what did you do next?". |
| "Talk me through your workflow." | ✅ Good | Many answers in one: how they spend days, tools, people, constraints, what you'd have to integrate with. | Use after rapport; keep silent while they talk. |
| "What else have you tried?" | ✅ Good | Current solution, cost, what they love/hate, how painful switching would be. | If "nothing" — ask why not (no care = not a customer). |
| "Would you pay X for a product which did Y?" | ❌ Bad | Adding a number doesn't help; hypothetical, about your idea. | Ask what they do now; or ask for real money. |
| "How are you dealing with it now?" | ✅ Good | Workflow + price anchor (a £100/month workaround vs £120k of agency fees puts you in different markets). | Decide which spend you want to replace. |
| "Where does the money come from?" | ✅ Good (must-ask in B2B) | Reveals budget owner and who can torpedo the deal; seeds a repeatable sales roadmap. | You're often not talking to the budget holder — find them. |
| "Who else should I talk to?" | ✅ Good — end every conversation with it | Lines up conversations; leads multiply via intros. If they decline, you were too formal/pitchy or they don't care. | Always ask. |
| "Is there anything else I should have asked?" | ✅ Good (a crutch you'll outgrow) | Gives them an excuse to politely fix your line of questioning. | Closing question until you know the industry. |

## 2. Opening broad (Ch. 3 "Look before you zoom")

- "What are your big goals and focuses right now?" — products that solve problems on this list are far more likely to be bought.
- "What are your big problems right now?" (generic), or "…with <area>?" only if you're certain the area is a top-3 problem for this segment.
- "What are the 3 big things you're trying to fix or improve right now?"
- Demographic warm-ups ("How often do you go to…?") are fine to orient yourself, but give no insight alone.

### Does-this-problem-matter questions
How seriously do you take it? Do you make money from it / have you tried to make more? How much time do you spend on it per week? Any major aspirations for it? Which tools/services do you use? What are you already doing to improve it? What are the 3 big things you're trying to fix right now?

## 3. Digging

**Into feature requests:** "Why do you want that?" · "What would that let you do?" · "How are you coping without it?" · "Do you think we should delay the launch to add it, or could it come later?" · "How would that fit into your day?"

**Into emotional signals:** "Tell me more about that." · "That seems to really bug you — I bet there's a story here." · "What makes it so awful?" · "Why haven't you been able to fix this already?" · "You seem pretty excited about that — it's a big deal?" · "Why so happy?" · "Go on."

## 4. Anchoring fluff — conversion patterns

| Fluff you hear | Anchor with |
|---|---|
| "I always / usually / never…" | "When was the last time that happened?" → "Talk me through it" |
| "I would definitely buy that" | "What do you do about it today? When did you last look for something? What did you find?" → ask for a commitment (see `conversation-process.md`) |
| "I might use it someday" | "Have you ever tried anything like that? Why not download it now?" |
| "Someone should make an X!" | "Have you looked for an X?" — if no, they're a complainer, not a customer |
| "I'm an Inbox-Zero person" | "What does your inbox look like right now? When did it last fall apart? How did you handle it?" |

## 5. Rewrite patterns (bad → good)

| Defect | Bad | Good (Mom-Test-proof) |
|---|---|---|
| About your idea | "Do you like our app?" | "How do you handle <problem> today?" |
| Opinion | "Do you think X is a good idea?" | "Tell me about the last time you needed X." |
| Future / hypothetical | "Would you use…?" / "Would you pay…?" | "What did you do the last time?", "What does that cost you today?" |
| Generic | "What do you usually do?" | "What's the last thing you did? Show me." |
| Leading | "Don't you hate it when…?" | "Tell me about the last time <situation> came up." |
| Pitch disguised | "We're building X — how would that fit in your day?" (before knowing the problem matters) | Keep the idea out until a strong signal; then ask it |
| Feature wish | "What features do you want?" | "Why do you want that? How are you coping without it?" |
| Closed/rank | "Rank convenience, cost, novelty…" (assumes they care) | "What are your top problems right now?" |

**Spanish equivalents (skill convenience):**
- ❌ "¿Te parece buena idea?" → ✅ "Cuéntame cómo lo resuelves hoy."
- ❌ "¿Comprarías una app que hiciera X?" → ✅ "¿Cuándo fue la última vez que te pasó X? ¿Qué hiciste?"
- ❌ "¿Cuánto pagarías por X?" → ✅ "¿Cuánto te cuesta hoy resolver X (dinero y tiempo)? ¿Hay presupuesto?"
- ❌ "¿Qué haces normalmente?" → ✅ "¿Qué fue lo último que hiciste? Enséñamelo / llévame paso a paso."
- ✅ "¿Por qué te molestas en hacerlo así?" · "¿Qué implicaciones tuvo?" · "¿Qué más has probado?" · "¿De dónde sale el presupuesto?" · "¿Con quién más debería hablar?" · "¿Hay algo que debería haberte preguntado?"

## 6. Questions by purpose (assemble a script from these)

| Purpose | Candidate questions |
|---|---|
| Their goals and priorities | big goals/focus now; the 3 things they're trying to fix; what would make this quarter a success |
| Does the problem exist and matter? | last time it happened; implications; how often; what it costs (time/money); what they did about it |
| Current solution / alternatives | how are you dealing with it now; what else have you tried; what do you love/hate; what does it cost; how painful would switching be |
| Workflow & context | talk me through the last time; show me; who is involved; which tools; what do you do before/after |
| Motivation behind a request | why do you want that; what would it let you do; how are you coping without it |
| Money & buying process (esp. B2B) | where does the money come from; who else decides; who could torpedo it; what was bought last time and how; what happened with the last similar purchase |
| Segment fit | what are they already doing to solve it; where else does this person hang out; do all people like them have this goal |
| Closing the conversation | who else should I talk to; anything I should have asked; next step / commitment (see `conversation-process.md`) |

## 7. Questions to avoid / treat with care

"Would you ever…", "Do you ever…" (OK only as a springboard to a concrete past example), "What do you usually…", "Do you think you…", "Might you…", "Could you see yourself…", "On a scale of 1 to 5, how much would you…", "Do you like it?", "Can I interview you?", "Can I get your opinion on what we're doing?". These aren't toxic — the *answers* are useless; the mistake is valuing the answers. They can help tempo when transitioning to concrete questions.
